Step 1: Define the IndustryPer Dr. Sreekumar: "Be specific. Bounded by product/service, geography, and customer type."

❌ Bad Definition

"Digital payments" or "Fintech" — too broad. Includes consumer wallets, PoS hardware, BNPL, insurance, wealth-tech.

✅ Our Definition

The Indian B2B online payment aggregation and gateway industry, focused on digital checkout, routing, and settlement infrastructure for merchants and enterprises. Excludes offline PoS, consumer UPI wallets (PhonePe/GPay), traditional acquiring bank networks, and standalone lending/BNPL products.

DimensionIncludedExcluded
Product/ServicePayment gateways, aggregation APIs, merchant checkout, payouts, settlementOffline PoS terminals, ATMs, SWIFT, BNPL credit lines
GeographyIndia (domestic)International markets (SE Asia, Middle East)
CustomerB2B — Merchants, enterprises, startups, platformsB2C — Consumers using PhonePe/GPay/Paytm

Industry vs. Market (Key Distinction from Course)

"We belong to an industry but compete in a market"

Industry: Group of companies producing similar services — all five payment gateways share a similar resource configuration (API infrastructure, banking partnerships, RBI licenses).
Market: Where buyers (merchants) and sellers (PGs) transact. Razorpay competes in the Indian startup/SME merchant market, while BillDesk competes in the government/utility biller market. Same industry, different markets.

Market Structure Classification

This industry sits between Oligopoly (Cournot) and Bertrand Competition

5 firms control >75% of volume — classic oligopoly. However, the commodity nature of checkout routing and zero-MDR regulation push behavior toward Bertrand competition (price-based), where firms compete MDR rates down to near-zero, transferring profits to buyers (merchants). This is why industry margins are negative — economic profits are being driven toward zero, consistent with the theoretical prediction for Bertrand oligopoly.

Step 4: Five Forces Structural Analysis"The purpose is not to declare attractive/unattractive but to understand drivers of profitability" — Porter

1. Rivalry Among Incumbents EXTREMELY HIGH

Intensity: 5 balanced competitors (no one >31% share). Industry growth decelerating (UPI volume growth 106%→42%). High exit barriers (banking partnerships, regulatory licenses, merchant lock-in).

Basis: Low product differentiation (checkout is commodity). High fixed costs (server infrastructure) + low marginal costs per transaction. "Rivalry gravitates solely to price" — MDR undercutting to 10–25 bps for enterprise. This directly transfers profits from PGs to merchants.

Numbers: Razorpay revenue declined -1.82% YoY in FY24 despite volume growth = pure price-based competition.

2. Bargaining Power of Buyers (Merchants) VERY HIGH

Enterprise merchants (Swiggy, Zomato, Flipkart) = large portion of output. Products undifferentiated/standardized (REST APIs). Low switching costs. Orchestration layers (Juspay) let merchants route across 3-4 PGs in real-time. Credible backward integration threat: large platforms could build their own payment rails.

3. Bargaining Power of Suppliers (Banks/NPCI) VERY HIGH

Dominated by few: Visa, Mastercard set network fees. HDFC/ICICI/SBI capture 70–80% of card MDR as interchange. NPCI is monopsonistic clearing rail — dictates UPI rules with zero flexibility. High switching costs for PGs (banking integrations take 6–12 months). No substitutes for acquiring bank rails.

4. Threat of Substitutes MODERATE-HIGH

UPI Intent/AutoPay bypass card rails entirely. CBDC (e-Rupee) = potential future substitute. Account Aggregator (AA) framework enables direct bank-debit. Large enterprises building host-to-host bank APIs for payouts. Subject to trends improving price-performance: WhatsApp Pay bypasses traditional PG checkout.

5. Threat of New Entry MODERATE-HIGH

Barriers: RBI PA license (₹15–25 Cr net worth), PCI-DSS Level 1, SOC-2 Type II, CERT-In audits, data localization. Learning curve advantages (BillDesk's 20-year biller network). 12–15 month RBI moratoriums. But: Stripe ($95B valuation) and Adyen could enter with scale and capital. Expected retaliation is high.

Five Forces → Profitability Link

Very High Rivalry + Very High Buyer Power + Very High Supplier Power = Profits squeezed from both sides. PGs are caught between powerful banks/networks (suppliers) and powerful merchants (buyers), competing on price in a commodity market. This is why the weighted average margin is -12.67%. The only escape: build switching costs that neutralize buyer power, or exit the commodity layer entirely.

"Rivalry is especially destructive to profitability if it gravitates solely to price because price competition transfers profits directly from an industry to its customers" — Porter

Dual Advantage Analysis

"Creation of Dual Advantage is rare – it requires the firm to break the conventional tradeoff between willingness to pay and costs"

No firm in this industry holds a dual advantage. Razorpay has differentiation (premium platform) but not cost leadership. Infibeam has cost efficiency but not differentiation. BillDesk has niche focus margins but is shrinking. The industry is too competitive and too regulated for any firm to sustainably achieve both low cost and high differentiation simultaneously.

Strategic Choices (Not Operating Imperatives)

Test: "Is the opposite of your choice stupid on its face?"

"We chose to build a full Financial OS rather than remain a pure payment gateway" — This IS a strategic choice because the opposite (remaining a pure PG, like Cashfree) is a legitimate, non-stupid alternative.
"We chose to prioritize developer experience" — This is an operating imperative (the opposite IS stupid). Good DX is table stakes. The strategic choice is what you build on top of that DX.

Competing in the Age of AI

AI-Driven Operating Model: Scale, Scope & Learning

Razorpay's AI advantage: Unlike traditional bakeries (diminishing returns to scale), Razorpay's AI-driven fraud detection, smart routing, and credit underwriting continuously improve with more transaction data. Every additional transaction makes the model better — returns on scale climb to "previously unheard-of levels."

Infibeam's counter-move: Rebranded to AvenuesAI. Launched PayCentral.ai — agentic payments where AI agents autonomously initiate, negotiate, verify, and settle transactions. This is a bet that the PG checkout interface becomes obsolete in an AI-first world.

Risk: Algorithmic bias in credit underwriting. Data privacy regulations. Cybersecurity vulnerability at scale.

Assessment: The PG industry is undergoing Intermediating Change

Core activities (routing transactions through a gateway) are being threatened by direct UPI rails, but core assets (merchant relationships, data, brand trust) remain valuable. This is why Razorpay's strategy of shifting from activities (checkout) to assets (data, ecosystem) is strategically correct. Firms that cling to the checkout activity will be disintermediated. Firms that leverage their asset base (merchant data) into lending, SaaS, and platform services will survive.

Strategic Implications

  • BillDesk: In Progressive change — can maintain status quo but faces slow decline as digital-first billers emerge.
  • Razorpay: Navigating Intermediating change correctly — shifting from gateway activities to platform assets (data, ecosystem).
  • PayU: Attempting to navigate via lending but execution failures (₹3.5K Cr write-downs) suggest difficulty.
  • Infibeam: Attempting to escape domestic intermediation via international expansion and AI pivot — high risk, high reward.
  • Cashfree: Most vulnerable — core payout activity may be disintermediated by direct bank APIs. Must build unique assets quickly.
